# Gilded Ambush

A design philosophy for the port's icon: one image that reads at 16 pixels and
rewards a look at 256.

**Space and form.** The composition is a single diagonal: a dark human
silhouette rising from the lower left, gold letterforms stacked on the upper
right, the two meeting along a line of tension rather than touching. Nothing
is centred. The figure is built from a few large shapes (helmet, brow,
shoulder, the rifle's rising line) so that at thumbnail size it collapses into
a recognisable mass, while at full size each shape carries a hand-cut edge.
Every contour is placed with the patience of a stone cutter; the work must
look as if it took countless hours, the product of deep expertise.

**Colour and material.** Two materials only. Jungle: layered greens sinking
into near-black, lit from behind by a pale humid sky, with camouflage broken
into torn patches of olive, moss and shadow. Metal: a beveled gold that
catches light on its upper faces and burns to amber on its lower faces, each
letter a small ingot with a dark crease between highlight and shade. The gold
must feel machined and heavy, the greens must feel wet. Colour is graded, never
flat, but the gradients are quiet and few.

**Scale and rhythm.** The lettering is oversized against the icon's frame; it
is allowed to press against the edges the way a title presses against a
screen. Two words, two rows, the second slightly wider than the first, so the
stack leans. The figure answers with a counter-rhythm of round shapes against
the letters' hard angles.

**Composition and balance.** The dark mass sits low and left; the bright mass
sits high and right; the sky's glow bridges them. A thin band of darker earth
along the bottom anchors the whole. The frame is a rounded square with a
subtle inner vignette, so the image reads as an object rather than a crop.

**Hierarchy.** Gold first, figure second, jungle third. At the smallest size
only the gold and the silhouette survive; that is intended. Every detail that
does not serve those two masses is cut. The result should look meticulously
crafted, painstakingly balanced, unmistakably the work of someone at the top
of their field.
